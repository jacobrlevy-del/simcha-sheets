Lighting the Menorah on Friday Night: Chanukah and Shabbat 2026

In 2026, Chanukah has not one but two Friday nights. The first candle is lit on Friday 4 December and the eighth on Friday 11 December, both just before Shabbat begins. In between, Saturday night 5 December brings the question of Havdalah and the menorah. This guide walks through the common practices so you can plan ahead. Customs genuinely vary between communities, so treat this as a practical overview and ask your rabbi about your own family’s practice.

The order: menorah first, then Shabbat candles

Lighting a flame isn’t done on Shabbat, so the Chanukah lights need to be kindled before Shabbat begins. The widely followed practice is to light the menorah first and the Shabbat candles afterwards. Lighting Shabbat candles is generally understood as welcoming Shabbat, so once they’re lit, it’s considered too late to light the menorah.

A typical Friday sequence looks like this:

  1. Finish cooking and switch off or set up anything that needs to be ready for Shabbat.
  2. Many have the custom to pray Mincha (the afternoon service) before lighting the menorah on Friday, where that’s practical. Ask your rabbi what’s best for you.
  3. Light the menorah with the usual blessings. On Friday 4 December, the first night, the Shehecheyanu blessing is added.
  4. Then light the Shabbat candles, at or before your local candle-lighting time.

Leave yourself a few minutes’ margin. Rushing at the last moment with matches and a full table is exactly when accidents happen. Many authorities also say not to light too early on Friday afternoon, and give an earliest time; check with your rabbi or local calendar.

Candle size and burn time

On an ordinary night, the Chanukah lights are commonly required to burn for at least half an hour after nightfall. On Friday, you light before sunset, so the lights have to last much longer than usual to reach that point: the time until sunset, the time until nightfall, plus the half hour.

The small coloured candles sold in most Chanukah boxes often burn out well before then. Practical options include:

  • Larger candles: some use Shabbat candles or other longer-burning candles in the menorah on Fridays.
  • Oil: olive oil with floating wicks or pre-filled oil cups, with enough oil for the longer burn.
  • A test run: light one of your candles on a weeknight and time it, so you know what you’re working with.

Make sure the larger candles actually fit your menorah, and buy them early. Thicker candles are often what runs out in shops close to the holiday.

During Shabbat

Once Shabbat begins, the menorah isn’t moved, adjusted or relit, and the lights aren’t used for reading or other practical purposes. That makes placement before lighting especially important on Friday. Choose a spot where it can safely burn out on its own: away from curtains, doors, drafts and busy walkways, on a stable, heat-proof surface. Many families put a metal tray or foil underneath to catch drips.

On Shabbat itself, the Al HaNissim prayer is added, and Hallel is said in shul as on every day of Chanukah.

Saturday night: Havdalah and the menorah

On Saturday 5 December, the second candle is lit only after Shabbat has ended. Wait for your local time for the end of Shabbat. Many people say Atah Chonantanu in the evening prayer, or the phrase Baruch hamavdil bein kodesh l’chol, before doing any work such as lighting.

Communities differ on what comes first. In many shuls, the menorah is lit before Havdalah. At home, some families also light the menorah first, while others make Havdalah first and then light. Both practices have sources and long traditions behind them, so follow your family’s custom or ask your rabbi.

The second Friday, 11 December, follows the same pattern as the first: menorah first, all eight lights, long-burning candles or oil, then Shabbat candles. On Saturday 12 December, the last day of Chanukah (often called Zot Chanukah), the festival ends at nightfall, so there is no menorah to light that night.

Safety on a Shabbat Chanukah

  • Never leave burning candles unattended, and make sure an adult is in the house the whole time they’re lit.
  • Keep the menorah away from curtains, paper decorations, tablecloth edges and anything children could pull.
  • Stand the menorah on a heat-proof tray, especially with bigger candles or oil.
  • Keep matches and lighters out of children’s reach.
  • Check your smoke alarm is working before Chanukah starts.
  • Think about pets, coats on hooks and open windows before you light, because on Shabbat you won’t be moving the menorah.

Plan both Fridays in advance

Look up your local Shabbat candle-lighting and end-of-Shabbat times for 4, 5, 11 and 12 December, and write them down. Set out the menorah, candles or oil, and matches early on Friday. If you’re hosting, prepare food that can sit on a hot plate or in a warm oven according to your usual Shabbat practice; latkes can be fried ahead and reheated before Shabbat.
