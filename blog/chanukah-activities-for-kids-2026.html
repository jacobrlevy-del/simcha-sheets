Chanukah Activities for Kids: 8 Nights of Ideas for 2026

Eight nights is a lot of evenings to fill, and the menorah only takes a few minutes to light. The trick is to have one small, simple plan ready for each night, something that takes 15 to 30 minutes and needs almost nothing you don’t already have. This year Chanukah runs from the evening of Friday 4 December to Saturday 12 December 2026, and both the first and eighth nights fall on a Friday. So the plan below keeps those two nights Shabbat-friendly, with no crafts, screens or flames after candle-lighting.

Night 1, Friday 4 December: a menorah-light Shabbat dinner

The menorah is lit before the Shabbat candles tonight, so the table glows twice over. Keep it calm: at dinner, go round and ask everyone to share one good thing, one small “miracle”, from the past year. Little ones can draw theirs on Friday afternoon, before Shabbat starts, and bring it to the table. Our guide to lighting the menorah on Friday night explains the order and the candles you’ll need.

Night 2, Saturday 5 December: dreidel tournament

Once Shabbat is over and the second candle is lit, bring out the dreidels. Give everyone the same number of chocolate coins, raisins or buttons, and play a few rounds with a proper scoreboard. Older kids can be the official scorekeeper. Our free Dreidel Game Night sheet has the rules and a scoreboard ready to print.

Night 3, Sunday 6 December: family latke kitchen

Sunday is a natural cooking night. Children can wash potatoes, crack eggs, mix and set out toppings, while an adult handles the grater and the hot oil. Talk about why we eat foods fried in oil on Chanukah. Our crispy potato latkes recipe walks you through it step by step.

Night 4, Monday 7 December: pass-the-story night

Tell the Chanukah story together, one sentence each, going round the table. Younger children can add sound effects or act out the Maccabees. Afterwards, ask: what does courage look like for a kid today?

Night 5, Tuesday 8 December: giving night

Many families set aside part of their Chanukah gelt for tzedakah. Tonight, let the children choose a cause together, count out what they’d like to give, and decide who they’d most like to help. A jar on the table, labelled in their own handwriting, makes it feel real.

Night 6, Wednesday 9 December: window craft night

Rosh Chodesh Tevet begins this evening. Make paper menorahs, Magen David cut-outs or a colouring page and hang them in the window, near where the menorah stands. Ask the kids why we put the menorah where people outside can see it.

Night 7, Thursday 10 December: share-the-light call

Video-call grandparents, cousins or an older neighbour and light at the same time, or let the kids show off the menorah and sing together. It’s a lovely way to include people who are on their own this year.

Night 8, Friday 11 December: eight-lights reflection

All eight candles are lit before Shabbat. Before you light the Shabbat candles, take a minute to look at the full menorah together. At dinner, everyone names their favourite night and one thing they’d like to do again next year.

Low-prep Chanukah crafts

  • Paper-plate menorah: nine strips of coloured paper glued onto a plate, with tissue-paper flames added one per night.
  • Decorated gelt bags: plain paper bags with stickers and drawings, ready for coins or treats.
  • Salt-dough dreidels: shape, bake until hard, then paint the four letters on the sides.
  • Window stars: fold and cut paper into six-pointed stars and tape them to the glass.

Save the crafts for weeknights, since this year both Friday nights and Saturday daytime are Shabbat.

Games beyond the dreidel

  • Gelt hunt: hide chocolate coins around one room and give small hints like “warmer” or “colder”.
  • Chanukah charades: act out latkes, the shamash, a spinning dreidel or the Maccabees.
  • Candle count quiz: how many candles do you need for all eight nights? (Answer: 44, counting the shamash each night.)
  • Freeze dance: play Chanukah songs and freeze when the music stops.

Giving ideas for kids

Chanukah is about sharing light, and children often love being the givers. They can make cards for a care home, pack a bag of toys or books they’ve outgrown, bake doughnut bites for a neighbour, or put part of their gelt towards a cause they choose themselves.
